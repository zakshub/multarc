# MULTARC — Visual Style Bible v0.1

**Status:** Production Direction  
**Scope:** Shared visual language for comics and later 2D animated adaptation  
**Purpose:** Define a distinct MULTARC visual system before final character and environment artwork begins.

---

## 1. Core Direction

MULTARC uses an **original adult 2D science-fiction cartoon language** designed for long-form comics, repeated character consistency, dimensional worldbuilding, horror, mystery, action, satire, drama, and later AI-assisted animation.

The visual system may learn from the production strengths of adult sci-fi animation, but it must not directly imitate the anatomy, facial construction, portal language, color system, or recurring motifs of any existing series.

The guiding principle is:

> **Reality may become extremely strange. The frame must remain immediately readable.**

---

## 2. Character Rendering

### Line System
- confident outer contour
- medium-to-bold silhouette line
- lighter internal detail lines
- clean separation between face, hair, costume, and props
- avoid sketchy or painterly line instability in production sheets

### Anatomy
- simplified from realistic anatomy
- recognizable human proportions
- no superhero exaggeration by default
- no oversized heads or tiny bodies unless a specific character design requires it
- hands must remain readable and practical for repeated animation poses

### Face Construction
- identity-preserving simplification
- expressive eyebrows and eyes
- readable mouth shapes
- limited but clear facial planes
- strong silhouette differences between characters
- no copied Rick and Morty eye or mouth construction
- no anime facial proportions unless intentionally used for a specific world or satire

### Expressions
Expressions should be broad enough for animation but grounded enough to support horror and drama.

Priority expression range:
- neutral
- skeptical
- focused
- amused
- irritated
- angry
- afraid
- shocked
- exhausted
- emotionally blank
- grief
- suspicion

---

## 3. Shading and Surface

### Primary Shading
- flat local color
- one controlled cel-shadow layer
- optional second shadow only for night, horror, or dramatic scenes
- highlights used sparingly

### Texture
- characters remain relatively clean
- environment may carry more texture
- grime, wear, cracks, rust, dust, paper, concrete, and old paint are useful story signals
- texture must not destroy silhouette clarity

### Skin
- no photoreal pores
- no glossy cinematic skin
- no live-action rendering
- tone variation should be graphic rather than photographic

---

## 4. Color Philosophy

### Default
- grounded, restrained base palettes
- stronger accents reserved for anomaly, technology, danger, or dimensional events

### Horror
Horror should not depend on black-and-red clichés.

Possible horror palettes:
- sickly fluorescent green
- stale hospital beige
- sodium orange
- dead blue
- washed concrete gray
- dim warm domestic light
- overexposed white
- normal-looking daylight used in impossible spaces

### Dimensional Events
Each major dimensional system may have its own palette logic, but avoid using glowing neon portals as a universal shorthand.

---

## 5. Background and Environment Density

Characters remain visually simple enough for animation.

Backgrounds may be more detailed, especially when the environment itself carries narrative information.

Environmental storytelling can use:
- signage
- architecture
- wiring
- maintenance markings
- old paint
- utility labels
- maps
- door numbers
- public notices
- security systems
- posters
- discarded objects
- structural damage
- hidden symbols

Every major environment should teach the reader something.

---

## 6. Horror Language

MULTARC horror should come primarily from:
- impossible architecture
- violated expectations
- silence
- repetition
- missing information
- altered scale
- wrong spatial relationships
- human behavior that is almost normal
- records that contradict lived experience
- objects appearing in the wrong context
- spaces that continue beyond plausible geometry

Avoid relying only on:
- gore
- monsters jumping at camera
- dark corridors with no conceptual rule
- generic demonic imagery

---

## 7. Science-Fiction Language

Science fiction should appear as systems, not decoration.

Technology should have:
- purpose
- operating logic
- failure state
- visible wear
- physical interface logic
- continuity across appearances

Dimensional phenomena should have:
- observable rules
- consistent consequences
- specific visual signatures

---

## 8. Comedy / Satire

Comedy is permitted but not mandatory.

When present, humor should come from:
- system logic
- character contradiction
- social behavior
- institutional absurdity
- visual background details
- literal consequences of strange rules

The franchise is multi-genre and must never become visually trapped in one comedic tone.

---

## 9. Panel Readability

### Default Panel Goal
The reader should understand:
1. where they are
2. who is present
3. what changed
4. where attention should go

### Action
- clear motion direction
- readable silhouettes
- limited unnecessary background noise

### Horror
- negative space is encouraged
- small visual inconsistencies can be hidden in otherwise normal panels
- repeated compositions can be used to expose changes

### Mystery
- clues must be visible enough to reward rereading
- important evidence should not depend on unreadable micro-detail

---

## 10. Animation Compatibility

Every recurring character must be reducible into:
- front
- side
- rear
- 3/4
- mouth states
- eye states
- expression library
- hand states
- walk cycle logic
- seated pose
- running pose
- object interaction poses

Every recurring environment must have:
- establishing view
- map / layout
- reusable angles
- lighting states
- damage / anomaly states

---

## 11. Character 001 Application

CHR-001 at launch age 29–30 should use:
- lean-to-medium body
- medium-length dense dark hair
- short full beard
- thick eyebrows
- large expressive eyes
- elongated oval face
- practical charcoal T-shirt
- earthy brown practical trousers
- brown low-profile footwear
- rugged square watch as a recurring identity anchor
- no superhero costume

The mature age 38–40 version may evolve into:
- longer hair
- heavier beard
- natural grey progression
- more worn uniform
- stronger fatigue and life-history signals

---

## 12. Hard Avoid List

- photoreal final character art
- painted live-action likeness
- generic superhero anatomy
- direct Rick and Morty facial construction
- direct Marvel costume language
- generic anime shorthand
- random neon sci-fi
- arbitrary cyberpunk detail
- texture-heavy AI noise
- inconsistent character proportions
- unexplained style changes between issues

---

## 13. Approval Gate

No final comic artwork should begin until:
1. this visual direction is accepted
2. CHR-001 launch model sheet is approved
3. CHR-001 age progression sheet is approved
4. required environment sheets for Story 01 are approved

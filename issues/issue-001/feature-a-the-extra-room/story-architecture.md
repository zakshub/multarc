# Issue 001 — Feature A Story Architecture v0.1

**Character:** CHR-001  
**Working Feature Title:** THE SURVEYOR  
**Story Title:** THE EXTRA ROOM  
**Length:** 10 pages  
**Primary Mode:** Liminal science-fiction mystery / horror-thriller  
**Status:** Development

## Logline

A building surveyor is called to verify a minor floor-plan discrepancy in an aging apartment block and discovers a bedroom that measures correctly from inside but has no physical volume from outside — and the longer he documents it, the less the building's records acknowledge that he entered.

## Narrative Function

This is not an origin story.

The reader meets CHR-001 already competent, already suspicious of impossible architecture, and already carrying unexplained history.

The story should establish method before mythology.

## Core Anomaly

Apartment 6B is listed as a two-bedroom unit.

The tenant insists it has always had three bedrooms.

The third room:
- has electrical power
- has ventilation
- contains ordinary furniture
- measures 3.2 m × 3.8 m internally
- cannot fit inside the building envelope
- does not appear on any approved plan
- has no external wall, even though the room has a window

The window shows the building's rear alley from an angle that physically cannot exist.

## Local Stakes

The tenant's teenage son has begun sleeping in the room.

Over the last week:
- school attendance records intermittently omit him
- family photos occasionally fail to load
- his name disappears from one utility account
- a neighbor insists the family has only one child

The mother thinks the building management is falsifying records.

CHR-001 suspects a Residual Architecture event.

## Character Objective

CHR-001 wants to prove the room exists, map it, and extract the boy without triggering deeper referential drift.

## Opposition

The anomaly itself is the main antagonist.

Secondary pressure:
- building management wants a quick compliance sign-off
- the family wants certainty
- every additional measurement destabilizes records further

## Turning Point

CHR-001 discovers that every time the room is measured from a different reference point, the apartment remains internally consistent while the rest of the floor plan shifts by a few centimeters.

The impossible room is not expanding.

The building is making space for it.

## Crisis

The boy enters the room to retrieve his phone.

The door closes.

The mother immediately asks CHR-001:

"Who are you waiting for?"

She no longer remembers having a son.

## Choice

CHR-001 can leave with proof of the anomaly and preserve his own stability, or enter after the boy and risk referential drift.

He enters.

## Climax

Inside, the room has become a corridor lined with multiple versions of the same bedroom.

Each doorway contains a slightly different arrangement of the boy's life.

CHR-001 refuses to navigate by visual memory.

He uses physical measurement, thread line, analog chalk marks, and timed knocks from outside to locate the version with the correct acoustic relationship to Apartment 6B.

He retrieves the boy.

## Resolution

The third room is gone.

The apartment is once again two bedrooms.

The mother remembers her son.

Most digital records restore.

One thing does not:

The building's visitor log shows that CHR-001 never entered the property.

## Final Hook

Outside, CHR-001 checks a small paper notebook.

On a page written years earlier is a hand-drawn symbol identical to a mark he found behind the impossible room's skirting board.

He closes the notebook.

No explanation.

## Theme

Proof can preserve an event, but proof cannot guarantee belonging.

## Emotional Beat

CHR-001 saves someone from the kind of erasure he failed to prevent at age 23.

The reader is not told this directly.

His reaction after the rescue should imply that the case was personally familiar.

## Visual Signature

The defining image of the story is:

A normal apartment bedroom whose window shows an exterior angle that cannot physically exist.

The second defining image is:

A corridor composed of repeating versions of the same bedroom, each subtly different.

## Animation Adaptation Potential

Estimated short-form adaptation:
6–9 minutes.

Animation should preserve:
- spatial contradiction
- investigative rhythm
- acoustic navigation
- record-erasure escalation

Comic panels should not be treated as final shot list.

# Character 001 — Narrative Core v0.1

**Character ID:** CHR-001  
**Working Feature Title:** THE SURVEYOR  
**Status:** Development / Proposed Canon  
**Primary Launch Age:** 29–30  
**Primary Story Mode:** Liminal science-fiction mystery with horror, conspiracy, thriller, and adventure capacity

---

## One-Sentence Hook

A forensic spatial surveyor discovers that some buildings contain rooms, corridors, stairwells, and volumes that cannot exist within their physical dimensions — and every time he enters one, reality becomes slightly less certain that he belongs in the world he came from.

---

## Core Character Engine

### Identity
A highly observant urban spatial surveyor who trusts measurements more than memory and records more than testimony.

He is not a superhero by default. His narrative value comes from perception, method, stubbornness, field competence, and the fact that he is willing to enter places most people instinctively avoid.

### Desire
To prove that impossible spaces follow rules and can be mapped.

At the personal level, he wants certainty: if something happened, he needs it to be provable.

### Need
To accept that not everything real can be preserved through control, documentation, or proof.

He must eventually learn that relationships, trust, and lived experience can remain meaningful even when evidence fails.

### Wound
At age 23, he survived an incident inside a building whose internal geometry contradicted its exterior dimensions.

A second person entered the anomaly with him.

He came back.

The other person did not.

Afterward, every conventional record of that person's presence was inconsistent, corrupted, missing, or dismissed as error.

He became obsessed with the possibility that reality can lose people without physically killing them.

### Fear
His deepest fear is not death.

It is **erasure**:

- being removed from records
- being forgotten by people who knew him
- losing evidence that an event occurred
- discovering that his own memories cannot be trusted
- becoming one of the anomalies he studies

### Belief
"If it can be measured, it can be understood. If it can be documented, it cannot be erased."

### False Belief
Perfect documentation can protect reality from uncertainty.

### Contradiction
He obsessively records places, events, measurements, and anomalies, but is personally private and leaves very little of himself in ordinary social life.

He wants proof that people cannot be erased while unintentionally making himself easy to erase.

### Competence
- exceptional spatial reasoning
- architectural and infrastructural literacy
- forensic observation
- pattern detection
- map reconstruction
- practical field improvisation
- patient evidence collection
- ability to remain functional under disorientation
- strong memory for physical layouts

### Vulnerability
- obsessive checking and re-measuring
- difficulty trusting subjective testimony
- chronic sleep disruption
- isolation
- tendency to enter danger alone because he considers other people "uncontrolled variables"
- psychological destabilization when two measurements are both demonstrably correct but mutually impossible

# Ordinary Life

At the beginning of the series, CHR-001 works in a field adjacent to structural surveying, building compliance, forensic inspection, insurance investigation, or infrastructure diagnostics.

His work naturally sends him into:

- old apartment buildings
- malls
- hospitals
- offices
- parking structures
- abandoned developments
- tunnels
- transit systems
- industrial facilities

This creates a grounded entry point for strange stories without requiring him to seek supernatural adventures deliberately.

He is initially hired to investigate mundane discrepancies:

- missing floor area
- impossible utility routes
- rooms absent from plans
- repeated tenant complaints
- elevators reporting impossible stops
- CCTV paths that do not match architecture
- structures with more interior volume than exterior volume

# The Fictional Phenomenon

## Working Name: Residual Architecture

Residual Architecture is the provisional term for physical spaces that exist locally but cannot be reconciled with the geometry, records, measurements, or causal history of the structure containing them.

These spaces are not automatically another universe.

They may represent:

- topological errors
- displaced physical volume
- overlapping realities
- abandoned timelines
- artificial spatial technology
- cognitive phenomena
- something not yet understood

The franchise does not explain the full cosmology at launch.

---

# First-Layer Rules

### Rule 1 — Contradictory Measurement
A genuine residual space can produce two or more measurements that are individually repeatable and physically valid but mutually impossible.

Example:

Exterior width: 18.2 m  
Interior usable width: 21.7 m

Both measurements remain correct.

### Rule 2 — Threshold Dependence
Residual spaces are usually accessed through ordinary architectural thresholds:

- doors
- elevator openings
- stair landings
- service corridors
- maintenance hatches
- tunnels
- fire exits

The threshold becomes significant only under specific spatial conditions.

### Rule 3 — Observation Instability
The space may change depending on:

- number of observers
- route taken
- sequence of doors opened
- time interval between measurements
- whether the space is being recorded or remembered

### Rule 4 — No Universal Map
A map can accurately document one traversal and fail on the next.

Patterns exist, but routes are not permanently fixed.

### Rule 5 — Exit Cost
Repeated exposure creates **referential drift**.

The traveler remains physically present, but external reality begins to lose certainty about them.

Early symptoms may include:

- camera metadata corruption
- incorrect timestamps
- messages disappearing from threads
- building logs failing to register entry
- acquaintances misremembering dates
- identification systems returning intermittent errors

Heavy exposure may escalate toward social and historical erasure.

# System Design

## Ability
CHR-001 can identify spatial contradictions that most people dismiss as architectural error, faulty memory, or bad data.

This is initially presented as expertise and sensitivity, not a conventional superpower.

## Rule
He must physically establish a contradiction through observation, measurement, route reconstruction, or repeated evidence before he can reliably recognize a residual threshold.

## Limit
He cannot summon residual spaces.

He cannot control where they lead.

He cannot assume two anomalies obey the same rules.

## Cost
Exposure causes referential drift.

The more deeply he enters impossible space, the less reliably ordinary reality records him.

## Scarcity
True residual spaces are rare enough to remain extraordinary but common enough to generate recurring cases.

## Failure
A false model can trap him inside a route that changes faster than he can reconstruct it.

## Exploit
Organizations may intentionally create measurement contradictions, falsify plans, manipulate observers, or use people with high spatial sensitivity as living detection instruments.

## Counter
Redundant human witnesses, analog records, physical markers, synchronized observation, and deliberately distributed evidence can temporarily resist referential drift.

None is perfect.

## Consequence
Every investigation creates a long-term tension:

The better he becomes at finding impossible spaces, the more likely reality is to stop recognizing him as belonging to ordinary space.

# Emotional Engine

The external mystery asks:

**What is wrong with this place?**

The internal mystery asks:

**How much of himself can he afford to lose in order to prove that what happened was real?**

The long-term character arc asks:

**If no record proves you existed, what still makes a life real?**

---

# Story Engine

Each story can begin with a concrete spatial anomaly.

Examples:

- an apartment has one extra bedroom that no tenant remembers renting
- an elevator opens onto a floor absent from every plan
- a hospital corridor becomes longer each time a specific patient dies
- a shopping mall contains a service hallway that exits into the same mall twenty years earlier
- a parking structure repeatedly produces vehicles that were never manufactured
- an office has a conference room whose occupants cannot be remembered after the door closes
- a subway maintenance tunnel connects two stations that are not on the same line
- a condemned building remains physically accessible after demolition

The stories do not all need to be horror.

The same engine can support:

- investigation
- thriller
- conspiracy
- science fiction
- action
- tragedy
- surrealism
- dark comedy
- adventure
- cosmic escalation

---

# Long-Term Expansion Potential

Residual Architecture can later connect organically to:

- hidden institutions
- experimental technologies
- alternate histories
- other protagonists
- entities adapted to impossible geometry
- civilizations that intentionally inhabit residual space
- dimensional travel
- cosmological structures

These elements must not be explained in the first story.

The first story should prove the character and phenomenon before revealing the larger universe.

# Age Progression Narrative Utility

## Age 23
The original incident.

Visual state:
- younger
- less controlled
- less professionally established
- emotionally unprepared for the anomaly

## Age 29–30
Primary launch period.

He has converted obsession into professional method.

This is the best balance between:
- competence
- unresolved damage
- physical mobility
- future growth

## Age 34–35
He has accumulated successful cases and referential damage.

His methods are better.

His certainty is worse.

## Age 38–40
The mature identity system becomes narratively meaningful.

By this stage, his permanent utilitarian uniform, long hair, mature beard, rugged watch, and physically economical presence should communicate someone who has stopped dressing for ordinary social expectations.

The mature version may be:
- a hardened investigator
- a missing-person specialist
- partially erased
- living outside normal institutions
- or functioning as a guide into impossible space

The exact future state remains open until continuity earns it.

---

# First Feature Requirement

The launch story must:

1. begin with a mundane professional problem
2. establish CHR-001 through behavior rather than exposition
3. demonstrate one impossible spatial rule
4. force him to choose between safety and proof
5. impose a small but undeniable referential cost
6. resolve the local case
7. leave the larger phenomenon unexplained
8. contain no multiverse exposition dump
9. work as a complete short story inside an anthology issue
10. leave enough narrative pressure for a second appearance
